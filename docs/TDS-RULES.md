# TDS interest: rules verified for the calculator

Checked 14 September 2026 for the TDS interest calculator at /tds-interest-calculator.
incometaxindia.gov.in blocked automated reading (HTTP 403 and "Access Denied") for every page tried, including the transition FAQ PDF.
Where that happened, the same text was read from Indian Kanoon, which reproduces the Act and Rules, and the official page is still linked.
Each point below says whether it was verified from statutory or departmental text, or only from secondary sources.

## 1. Rates and "part of a month"

Verified.
Section 201(1A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 charges simple interest "at one per cent for every month or part of a month" from the date tax was deductible to the date it is deducted.
It charges "one and one-half per cent for every month or part of a month" from the date tax was deducted to the date it is actually paid.
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/613881/ and https://indiankanoon.org/doc/726238/ (official page https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-201-1).
Section 398(3)(a) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 has the same two rates with the same "for every month or part of a month" wording and the same two periods.
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/43121040/ and the Act as amended by Finance Act 2026, https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/d/guest/income_tax_act_2025_as_amended_by_fa_act_2026-pdf
The Rules also say a fraction of a month counts as a full month: Rule 119A(b), Income-tax Rules, 1962.
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/105253759/

## 2. Amount on which interest is charged

Verified for the 1962 Rules.
Rule 119A(c) rounds the tax on which interest is calculated down to a multiple of Rs 100, ignoring any fraction of Rs 100.
The research notes cited this as Rule 119A(b); the rounding is in clause (c), and clause (b) is the part-month rule.
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/105253759/
Income-tax Rules, 2026: two independent rule mapping tables list Rule 269, "Procedure to be followed in calculating interest", as the rule corresponding to Rule 119A.
Sources: https://www.mayuragr.com/income-tax-rules-2026-mapping/ and https://taxbizmantra.com/income-tax-rules-2026-mapping-tool/
The text of Rule 269 was not read from an official copy, so the calculator treats it as unchanged from Rule 119A and says so.

## 3. Due dates for depositing TDS

Verified for the 1962 Rules.
Rule 30(1): a government office pays on the same day when tax is paid without an income-tax challan, and within seven days from the end of the month of deduction when paid with a challan.
Rule 30(2): other deductors pay by 30 April when the amount is credited or paid in March, and otherwise within seven days from the end of the month of deduction.
Rule 30(3) allows quarterly payment with the Assessing Officer's permission for some sections.
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/106635308/
Income-tax Rules, 2026: Rule 218 is "Time and mode of payment to Government account of tax deducted or collected at source or tax paid under section 392(2)(a)".
The rule title is shown on the department's page https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/rule-218-1 (read through the search index; the page itself returned 403).
The rule mapping tables above list Rule 218 as replacing Rules 30 and 37CA.
A reproduction of Rule 218 gives the same dates as Rule 30: same day or seven days from month end for government offices, 30 April for March and seven days from month end otherwise for other deductors, and 30 days from month end for challan-cum-statement cases (rent, immovable property, contract and professional payments, virtual digital assets).
Source (secondary): https://www.taxheal.com/rule-218-income-tax-rules-2026.html
The department's Tax Payments FAQ confirms the dates for March 2026: 30 April 2026 for non-government deductors, and 7 April 2026 for government deductors paying by challan.
It also names Rule 218 as corresponding to Rule 30.
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/tax-payments-faq
The department's TDS compliance FAQ (question 8) says tax deducted in March 2026 and deposited in May 2026 attracts interest at 1.5% a month from the date of deduction to the date of payment, with the due date 30 April 2026.
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/%20tds%20compliance-faq

## 4. Defaults straddling 1 April 2026

Partly verified.
The TDS compliance FAQ (question 1) says the 1961 Act governs a deduction when the earlier of credit or payment happened on or before 31 March 2026.
Source: https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/%20tds%20compliance-faq
Section 536(2)(b) of the 2025 Act keeps "any right, privilege, obligation or liability acquired, accrued or incurred under the repealed Income-tax Act".
Section 536(2)(g) says that for a proceeding relating to a tax year beginning before 1 April 2026, where a default in paying a sum due under that proceeding is made after commencement, the 2025 Act's provisions on interest payable by the assessee for default apply for the period after commencement.
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/31102361/
No official text was found that says whether interest for the months from April 2026 on a March 2026 deduction falls under section 201(1A) or section 398(3)(a).
The department's FAQs on interplay and transition (https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/81799/11848482/FAQs-on-Interplay-and-Transition.pdf) could not be read (HTTP 403).
The calculator uses this reading: the Act is chosen by the date tax was deductible (on or before 31 March 2026 is the 1961 Act, section 201(1A) and Rule 30; from 1 April 2026 is the 2025 Act, section 398(3)(a) and Rule 218).
Months are counted as one continuous period across 1 April 2026.
The page flags straddling rows and says the section for the later months is not settled.
The rates, the part-month rule and the Rs 100 rounding are the same in both Acts, so the amount does not change under either reading.

## 5. Rounding the final interest

Verified text, application to TDS interest not verified.
Section 288B of the 1961 Act: "Any amount payable, and the amount of refund due, under the provisions of this Act shall be rounded off to the nearest multiple of ten rupees", ignoring paise, with 5 and above going up.
Source: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1566795/ (official page https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-288b-59).
Section 516 of the 2025 Act rounds "any amount payable or refundable under this Act" to the nearest multiple of Rs 10 in the same way.
Source (reproduction): https://eztax.in/income-tax-act-2025/section-516 and the Act PDF linked in point 1.
Interest is an amount payable, so the wording covers it.
No official text or TRACES document was found showing whether CPC-TDS rounds 201(1A) interest per default, per statement or not at all.
The calculator shows the interest as calculated, and separately shows the total rounded to the nearest Rs 10, labelled with both sections and marked as not confirmed for TRACES demands.

## 6. Calendar months or 30-day months

Verified that TRACES uses calendar months.
The TRACES deductor FAQ on late payment says interest runs "@ 1.5% per month or Part of the month from the date of deduction+1 day till date of Deposit".
Its example: Rs 10,000 deducted 10 June 2023 and deposited 8 August 2023 is 3 months, Rs 450.
It adds that if tax is deducted on the last day of a month, that month is not counted.
Source: https://traces61contents.tdscpc.gov.in/en/faq-dedu-default-lp.html
That example only works with calendar months (59 days would be 2 months under 30-day months).
Reported rulings for 30-day months, not read in full:
UTI Mutual Fund v. DCIT, ITAT Mumbai, ITA No. 2295/Mum/2018, 1 July 2019, reported at https://taxguru.in/income-tax/interest-late-tds-payment-30-days-month-british-calendar-month.html
CIT v. Arvind Textile Mills, Gujarat High Court, Tax Appeal No. 2486 of 2009, 13 September 2011, as cited in the same report.
Ready Roti India Pvt. Ltd. v. CPC-TDS, ITAT Jaipur, ITA No. 105/JPR/2023, as reported in search results.
The calculator uses calendar months as the main answer, counting from the day after the start date to the end date, and shows 30-day months (days between the dates divided by 30, rounded up) alongside.
The TRACES FAQ describes only the late-payment period; the calculator applies the same counting to the late-deduction period.

## 7. Supported period and exclusions

The calculator supports dates from 1 April 2021.
Earlier periods are excluded because the Taxation and Other Laws (Relaxation and Amendment) Act, 2020 reduced interest for some 2020 due dates, and the history of Rule 30 before then was not reviewed.
It excludes challan-cum-statement deductions (30 days from month end under Rule 218(3) and the older Rule 30 sub-rules) and quarterly payment with the Assessing Officer's permission.
It does not cover TCS interest, late filing fees, penalties or section 398(2) relief where the payee has paid the tax.
